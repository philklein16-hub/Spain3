Neighbourhood Guide

El Albaicín — Granada's Oldest Quarter

Free walkWear good shoesUNESCO 1994

The Palacio de Santa Paula sits at the foot of the Albaicín — Granada's oldest neighbourhood, founded in the 11th century by the Zirid dynasty. The Albaicín predates the Alhambra. Its name likely derives from the Arabic Rabat al-bayyasin — the falconers' neighbourhood. The street layout is virtually unchanged since the medieval period: steep, labyrinthine, deliberately confusing — designed to disorient invaders. The whitewashed walls, cármenes (walled gardens), and hidden fountains are traces of Nasrid domestic architecture.

Key stops as you walk up:
• Iglesia de San Salvador — a church built directly over a mosque, original Moorish courtyard intact
• Mirador de San Nicolás — Granada's most famous viewpoint: Alhambra across the valley, Sierra Nevada behind, the city below

The Sacromonte, just beyond the Albaicín, is the Roma neighbourhood that gave birth to flamenco. Founded in the 16th century when Roma settlers carved homes into the hillside caves. The zambra — flamenco variation fusing Andalusian footwork with Arabic-origin movement — was born here. Hemingway and Michelle Obama have attended cave shows here.

Your evening tour tomorrow (Plaza Nueva 8 PM) follows these same streets — let the guide reveal the layers.

Stop 1 · 5:30 PM

Bodegas Castañeda

€15–€22/coupleTravesía Almireceros 1–3, Granada Centro2-min walk from Plaza Nueva · Open Fridays

The most legendary bar in Granada — a time capsule of dark wood, ceramic jugs, and barrels of wine stacked to the ceiling. Open since the 19th century. Order the house vermut or a Calicasas (their famous house wine blend). A free tapa arrives automatically — if it contains meat ask for a vegetarian alternative, which they accommodate readily.

For Sarah: aubergine with honey, patatas bravas, tortilla española, salmorejo — among their many plant-based options. Ask for a vegetarian tapa if the default is meat.

➜ 2-min walk to Plaza Nueva 13 → Los Diamantes.

Stop 2 · 6:10 PM

Los Diamantes

€12–€18/couplePlaza Nueva 13, on the square itselfOpen Fridays

Granada institution open since 1942, right on Plaza Nueva. Famous for its fresh fish and seafood tapas — Phil should order the cazón en adobo (fried fish) or gambas. The terrace on the square is the place to be in the early evening. Always busy with locals as well as visitors.

For Sarah: grilled fish of the day, tortilla española, fresh salads — ask for the fish tapa without shellfish preparation.

➜ 3-min walk to Calle Monjas del Carmen → Malvasía.

Stop 3 · 6:45 PM

Malvasía

€15–€25/coupleCalle Monjas del Carmen, Granada CentroOpen Fridays · Free tapa

The wine-lover's stop — 100+ Spanish wines by the glass, selection changing monthly. The kitchen makes tapas to order and adapts freely for vegetarians. Order one glass of something exceptional and let the staff guide you. The most refined of the three stops — a good note to end on before the tour.

For Sarah: made-to-order vegetarian tapas — aubergine, seasonal vegetables, tortilla, cheese. Ask the kitchen to make something vegetarian and they will.

ALTERNATIVE IF CROWDED: Bar Candela (Cuesta Gomez 2, corner of Plaza Nueva) — small friendly bar with vegetarian and vegan options, good wines and vermouth. Use as a substitute for any stop that has a long wait.

Optional Late Dinner · ~10:00 PM

Restaurante Pañero — Albaicín

€10–€20ppPlaza Aliatar, AlbaicínOpen Fri until 11:30pm · +34 667 32 22 48

The tour ends near Plaza Nueva ~10 PM — Pañero is a 10-minute walk up into the Albaicín from here (or a 5-min walk if your guide ends near the upper quarter). Sits on Plaza Aliatar — a small cobbled square in the heart of the Albaicín opposite the Colegiata del Salvador. Tables spread out under the trees, street musicians pass through in the evenings, locals out in force. It feels genuinely removed from the tourist circuit.

Proudly traditional Andalusian, affordable, 4.5 stars across 807 reviews. Honest note: reviews mixed on consistency — food and service praised by most, atmosphere and location are the main draw.

For Sarah: queso frito con tomate (fried cheese with tomato — signature dish), pipirrana (salad of diced cucumber, tomatoes, peppers and onion), Spanish tortilla, patatas bravas, seasonal vegetable tapas — vegetarian and vegan options confirmed on menu. Tell staff: sin carne, sin jamón.

Walk up Cuesta del Chapiz from Plaza Nueva — about 10 minutes through the lit Albaicín streets at night. A lovely way to end the evening. No reservation needed for late arrival — they seat walk-ins until 11 PM.
