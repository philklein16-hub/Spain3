Neighbourhood Guide

El Albaicín — Granada's Oldest Quarter

Free walkWear good shoesUNESCO 1994

The Palacio de Santa Paula sits at the foot of the Albaicín — Granada's oldest neighbourhood, founded in the 11th century by the Zirid dynasty. The Albaicín predates the Alhambra. Its name likely derives from the Arabic Rabat al-bayyasin — the falconers' neighbourhood. The street layout is virtually unchanged since the medieval period: steep, labyrinthine, deliberately confusing — designed to disorient invaders. The whitewashed walls, cármenes (walled gardens), and hidden fountains are traces of Nasrid domestic architecture.

Key stops as you walk up:
• Iglesia de San Salvador — a church built directly over a mosque, original Moorish courtyard intact
• Mirador de San Nicolás — Granada's most famous viewpoint: Alhambra across the valley, Sierra Nevada behind, the city below

The Sacromonte, just beyond the Albaicín, is the Roma neighbourhood that gave birth to flamenco. Founded in the 16th century when Roma settlers carved homes into the hillside caves. The zambra — flamenco variation fusing Andalusian footwork with Arabic-origin movement — was born here. Hemingway and Michelle Obama have attended cave shows here.

Your evening tour tomorrow (Plaza Nueva 8 PM) follows these same streets — let the guide reveal the layers.
