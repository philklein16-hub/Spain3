Midday · Madrid Welcome

Churros & Hot Chocolate — Chocolatería San Ginés

~€12/couplePasadizo de San Ginés 55-min walk from hotel

Madrid's most iconic café, open since 1894. Tucked in a narrow alley behind Plaza Mayor. Order the porras (larger, fluffier version of churros) with thick dark hot chocolate — dip, let it coat, eat immediately. Served 24 hours. The walls are lined with photos of Almodóvar, Picasso, and a century of Madrileños.

Avoid the overflow seating — when staff guide you to the secondary space, ask for the original interior with green velvet seats instead.

1:00 PM Walk

Plaza Mayor & the Habsburg Quarter

Free2-min walk from San Ginés

Plaza Mayor was commissioned by Philip III in 1617 and completed in two years. 237 balconies ring the square — during its heyday, all were sold to nobility who rented them out as viewing boxes for bullfights, royal proclamations, executions, and autos-da-fé. The equestrian statue at the centre is Philip III himself, cast in 1616.

Exit through the Arco de Cuchilleros (Knife-Sellers' Arch) in the southwest corner — down the steps into the labyrinthine medieval streets of La Morería, the oldest quarter of Madrid. From Plaza Mayor to El Retiro is a 25-min walk through the Paseo del Prado, or 1 stop on the Metro (Sol → Retiro).

5:30 PM Afternoon Walk

El Retiro Park — Royal Garden, Crystal Palace & Fallen Angel

FreeRowboat €6/30 minUNESCO World Heritage Site

Created in the 1630s as a private royal garden for King Felipe IV — closed to the public for 200 years until 1868. Enter through the Puerta de Alcalá on Plaza de la Independencia, the neoclassical triumphal arch commissioned by Carlos III in 1778.

Key stops: The Estanque Grande (great lake) — rent a rowboat and look back at the city skyline. The Crystal Palace — built 1887 in iron and glass for the Philippine Islands Exhibition, modelled on London's Crystal Palace, now a free Reina Sofía art space. The Fallen Angel statue — one of the world's only public monuments to Lucifer, positioned at exactly 666 metres above sea level. The Paseo de las Estatuas — 18th-century kings originally made for the Royal Palace roof, rejected as too heavy. Exit south along the Paseo del Prado.

1:00 PM Walk · After the Tour

Plaza de España

Free · 20-min visit5-min walk from Royal Palace

One of Madrid's grandest squares, anchored by the Monument to Cervantes (1929) — a stone obelisk with the bronze figures of Don Quixote on horseback and Sancho Panza beneath it. Cervantes wrote Don Quixote in two volumes (1605 and 1615) months before he died.

The two skyscrapers flanking the square — the Torre de Madrid (1957) and the Edificio España (1953) — were built during the Franco era as symbols of Spanish economic ambition. For 15 years the Torre de Madrid was the tallest reinforced concrete building in the world. Look west: the Templo de Debod is a 10-min walk through Parque del Oeste — a preview of Wednesday evening's sunset.

6:30 PM Tapas Crawl

La Latina Neighbourhood — Calle Cava Baja

€30–€45/couple tapas + drinks15-min walk from hotel

One of Madrid's oldest neighbourhoods, with roots in the 15th century. The name comes from the Hospital de La Latina, founded in 1499 by Beatriz Galindo — the scholar who tutored Queen Isabella I in Latin. Calle Cava Baja is the spine of the neighbourhood: a medieval street that once ran along the outer city wall (the cava — a ditch).

Stop 1: Taberna Txakolina (Cava Baja 26) — Basque pintxos, one glass of txakoli each.
Stop 2: Casa Lucas (Cava Baja 30) — roasted red pepper with goat cheese.
Stop 3: El Tempranillo (Cava Baja 38) — 300+ Spanish wines by the glass.

Keep it light — dinner is at 9:15 PM at Brach Madrid. Plaza de la Paja nearby is one of Madrid's most beautiful medieval squares.

Wednesday, July 29 — El Retiro Deep Dive, Barrio de las Letras & Reina Sofía

Cultural exploration day
9:00 AM

El Retiro Deep Dive — Rose Garden & Ahuehuete Tree

A second, slower visit while it's still cool. The Rosaleda — 4,000 rose bushes in geometric parterre beds, designed in 1915. The Parterre Francés near the Felipe IV gate — a formal French garden containing the Ahuehuete tree, a Mexican swamp cypress over 400 years old, one of the oldest trees in Madrid (Napoleon's soldiers are said to have hidden a cannon in its branches in 1808). The Palacio de Velázquez — a Moorish-influenced brick pavilion hosting free Reina Sofía exhibitions.

11:00 AM

Barrio de las Letras — Madrid's Golden Age Literary Quarter

Free walkSherry at La Venencia €3.50

Madrid's literary heart in the 16th–17th centuries — the Spanish Golden Age. Cervantes, Lope de Vega, Quevedo, and Calderón all lived within a few streets of each other. Cervantes lived and died on Calle Cervantes — plaque marks the spot. Lope de Vega lived around the corner on the same street.

Look down at the paving stones of Calle de las Huertas — lines of Spanish poetry embossed in brass. La Venencia on Calle Echegaray has been pouring sherry since 1929 — no photography, no cards, judgment if you order anything besides fino or manzanilla. The floor is covered in discarded napkins (tradition). One of the best bars in Spain.

✦ Golden Hour

Templo de Debod — 8:15 PM

Free · Interior booking madrid.es/debodreservasMetro L3/L10 to Plaza de España

A genuine 2nd-century BC Egyptian temple, dismantled stone by stone and brought to Madrid from southern Egypt in the 1970s. When Egypt built the Aswan High Dam, dozens of ancient monuments faced submersion. UNESCO called for help; Spain sent engineers who helped save the temples of Abu Simbel. As gratitude, Egypt gifted Spain an entire temple — 1,350 boxes of stone shipped down the Nile, then by cargo ship to Valencia, then by 90 trucks to Madrid. Reassembled and opened to the public in 1972. The oldest monument in Madrid.

The temple is deliberately oriented west — aligned with the setting sun, exactly as it stood on the Nile. As the sun descends behind the Casa de Campo (~9:15 PM in late July), the limestone turns gold, the reflecting pool mirrors the sky, and the Royal Palace silhouette appears on the horizon. Madrileños arrive with wine and blankets.

Summer interior hours: closes 7pm (last entry 6:30pm) — the outdoor viewpoint and reflecting pool are freely accessible all evening and are the best vantage point anyway.

Recommended · Walking + Taxis
Why walk
The JW Marriott is central — Prado, Reina Sofía, Royal Palace, and Retiro are all walkable (10–20 min). Use taxis for longer trips (Malasaña, Huertas after dinner).
Metro
12 lines, 287+ stations. Runs 6am–1:30am (Fri–Sat until 2am). Nearest stations to JW Marriott: Banco de España (L2) or Retiro (L9).

• Single ticket: €1.50–€2.00 (Zone A)

Key lines for you
L1 (light blue): Sol → Atocha → Retiro. L2 (red): Banco de España → Sol → Ópera. Buy at station machines — English language available.
